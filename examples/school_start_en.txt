Secondary schools should start classes no earlier than 9 a.m. During adolescence the body's internal clock shifts later, so teenagers struggle to fall asleep before 11 p.m. As a result, an 8 a.m. start leaves most of them chronically sleep-deprived. Districts that moved to later start times reported better attendance and fewer car accidents among young drivers. Opponents point out that later classes complicate bus routes and after-school sports. However, these logistical problems can be solved with better planning, whereas lost sleep harms students' health and learning every single day.
